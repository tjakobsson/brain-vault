> "Improving daily work is even more important than doing daily work."
>
> Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, and George Spafford, *The Phoenix Project*

Daily delivery can consume all available capacity while recurring delays and failures remain untouched. Reserving time to improve the way work is done makes later work easier, faster, or safer.

In DevOps, this belongs to the Third Way: continual experimentation and learning. It requires improvement to be treated as work, rather than something attempted after delivery is finished.

[[Improving flow belongs inside the Outcome Loop]] applies the same principle to outcome management.

## Sources

- Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, and George Spafford, *The Phoenix Project: A Novel About IT, DevOps, and Helping Your Business Win*, 2013.
- [The Three Ways: The Principles Underpinning DevOps](https://itrevolution.com/articles/the-three-ways-principles-underpinning-devops/), Gene Kim, 2012.

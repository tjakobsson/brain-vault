> "Improving daily work is even more important than doing daily work."
>
> Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, and George Spafford, *The Phoenix Project*

Delivery and improvement are not competing categories of work. A team must spend some of its capacity removing the constraints, delays, and recurring failures in its own delivery system. Otherwise today's work makes tomorrow's work no easier, and pressure to deliver keeps the system stuck.

In *The Phoenix Project*, this is part of the Third Way: continual experimentation and learning. Gene Kim's earlier account of the Three Ways makes the practical requirement explicit: allocate time to improve daily work. Because the quotation appears in a three-author book, the attribution belongs to Kim, Kevin Behr, and George Spafford.

Mik Kersten puts the same principle inside the management system. His Outcome Loop connects outputs to measured outcomes and makes the loop itself an object of improvement. Optimizing it means applying the Theory of Constraints to planning and delivery, finding what limits flow, and removing that constraint. Improvement is therefore a top-level activity of the loop itself, rather than maintenance to do after the outcome work is finished.

The distinction is useful:

- Doing the work moves one output toward an outcome.
- Improving the work shortens or strengthens every later pass through the loop.

This compounds. Shipping one more item adds one item. Removing a persistent constraint increases the value stream's future capacity to learn and deliver.
